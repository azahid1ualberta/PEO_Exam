# A6 7-Day Sprint (Exam: Nov 5)

A focused plan for **16-Civ-A6 Highway Design, Construction & Maintenance**,
built from the question-by-question check in
[`A6_BOOK_COVERAGE.md`](A6_BOOK_COVERAGE.md). Put B7 on hold until after Nov 5.

## The strategy in one paragraph

You must answer **4 or 5 questions**. Five question types appear in almost every
recent paper, and all five can be learned from **your Mannering textbook plus
Part II of the AASHTO 1993 Guide** (the book you just confirmed). Master these
five and every 2017–2019 paper has enough questions you can answer. Skip
everything else.

| # | Question type | Recent papers it appeared in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vertical curves and stopping sight distance** (crash reconstruction, sag/underpass, elevations) | **6 of 6** |
| 2 | **AASHTO flexible pavement** (traffic/ESAL, check or design, layer-by-layer from moduli) | **6 of 6** |
| 3 | **Curve safety: radius check + spiral length + clear zone** | 4 of 6 (every paper since May 2018) |
| 4 | **AASHTO rigid pavement on shallow bedrock** | 3 of 6 (the last three papers) |
| 5 | **Horizontal curves** (minimum radius, sight clearance, speed limit, stationing) | 3 of 6 |

**Skip:** Superpave/asphalt grading, frost and Granular Base Equivalency, the
plate-bearing test, earthwork, aggregates, soils tests, drainage. Each appeared
once or twice at most, and you can always choose other questions.

**Assumption:** about 5 hours a day. If you have less, see "If a day runs short"
at the end.

**Hold back May 2019 and Dec 2019.** Don't look at them until the mock days.
Every question you need in those two papers is one of the five types above,
which makes them the best rehearsal.

## Day 1: Vertical curves and stopping sight distance

**Read** (Mannering 5th ed., printed pages; add 14 for the PDF page):
- §2.9.4–2.9.6, p. 32–44. Braking to a stop and to an **impact speed**, effect of grade. Examples 2.8, 2.9, 2.12.
- §3.3.1, p. 50–58. Curve elevations, high/low point, K. Examples 3.1, 3.3.
- §3.3.2–3.3.3, p. 58–63. Crest curves; the general formula with any eye and object height is on p. 60. Examples 3.5, 3.7.
- §3.3.4 and §3.3.6, p. 63–76. Sag (headlight) and underpass. Examples 3.8 and 3.13.

**Memorize:** `FORMULA_SHEETS.md` §A1–A2, in SI: 0.278Vt + V²/254(f±G), L = AS²/658, L = AS²/(120+3.5S), underpass, curve elevations.

**Practise:**

  - May 2018 Q2(a): table on PDF p. 6.

## In the exam room

**First 10 minutes:** read every question, then pick in this order:
1. Vertical curve / crash reconstruction
2. AASHTO flexible (traffic and/or layer design)
3. AASHTO rigid
4. Curve safety. Even with a weak clear-zone or spiral, part (a) earns marks.
5. Horizontal curve

Avoid Superpave, frost, plate bearing and life-cycle cost unless you know them well.

**Time:** 5 questions → about 32 minutes each. 4 questions → about 40 minutes each. Leave 10 minutes to check.

**Write your assumptions** (design speed, reliability, drainage quality, and so on) in one line each. The papers invite this and mark it.

**Nomographs:** draw clean construction lines and hand the chart in with your booklet if the paper says to.

**Only the first 4 or 5 answers in the booklet are marked.** Never start an extra question.

## Exam-kit checklist

- Approved Casio or Sharp calculator, fresh battery, SOLVE practised
- Ruler or straightedge for the nomographs
- Pencils, eraser, pens
- ID and exam notice
- Aid sheet, only if your notice allows one (recent A6 papers said closed book; Dec 2017 allowed one sheet)

## If a day runs short

**Keep on every day:** the reading, plus one practice question of that day's type.

**Drop first:**
1. The second practice question of each day
2. Day 5's life-cycle cost
3. The short-answer bank on Day 4. Skim `FORMULA_SHEETS.md` §A10 on Day 7 instead.

**Never drop the two mock exams.** They tell you which of the five types still needs work.
