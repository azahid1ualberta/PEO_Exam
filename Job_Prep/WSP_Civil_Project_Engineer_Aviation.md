## 2. Likely questions and what to read, by theme

### Theme 5: Airfield technical knowledge (study this most)

| Likely question | What to read / know |
|---|---|
| "How do you set runway and taxiway widths, separations and slopes for a project?" | **TP 312 5th ed., Ch 1** (**Aircraft Group Number, AGN**, set by the design aircraft) and **Ch 3 Physical Characteristics** (runways, strips, safety areas, stopways, taxiways, holding positions, aprons), whose tables are indexed by AGN. Compare with the ICAO aerodrome reference code (Annex 14, Vol I) and the FAA Runway Design Code / Airplane Design Group / Taxiway Design Group (**AC 150/5300-13B**). |
| "What are declared distances? When do they change?" | **TP 312 Ch 2 Aerodrome Data** (declared distances TORA/TODA/ASDA/LDA, pavement strength). They change with displaced thresholds, e.g. during runway-end construction. |
| "A crane or building near the airport: how do you check it?" | **TP 312 Ch 4 Obstacle Management** (approach, take-off, transitional and outer surfaces). Also Transport Canada **TP 1247** (land use near aerodromes) and NAV CANADA land-use review. |
| "Which standard governs in Canada: TP 312, ICAO or FAA?" | TP 312 is the Canadian standard for certified airports (referenced from the Canadian Aviation Regulations). ICAO Annex 14 is the international baseline TP 312 aligns with. FAA ACs are US, but Canadian designers use them widely for pavement design (FAARFIELD) and construction specs. Say that you **follow the client's governing standard and document any deviation**. |
| "How is airfield pavement designed? How is it different from road pavement?" | **FAA AC 150/5320-6G** + the free **FAARFIELD** software: layered elastic (flexible) and 3-D finite element (rigid), aircraft traffic mix, gear configuration, lateral wander, and a **cumulative damage factor** instead of ESALs. **Link it to your PEO A6 AASHTO study** (§3). |
| "How is pavement strength reported?" | ICAO moved from **ACN/PCN to ACR/PCR** (**FAA AC 150/5335-5D**, "Reporting Airport Pavement Strength – PCR"). Canada has also used its own **ALR/PLR** (aircraft/pavement load rating) system, so know that it exists. |
| "How do you evaluate an existing runway before rehab?" | Pavement Condition Index (ASTM D5340; **FAA AC 150/5320-17A** rating manuals), falling/heavy-weight deflectometer testing, cores and borehole logs, friction testing, and drainage condition. This leads into mill-and-overlay vs reconstruction options. |
| "What's the civil scope in an airfield lighting project?" | **TP 312 Ch 5 Visual Aids** and **Ch 8 Aerodrome Lighting System Design and Operations**; FAA AC 150/5340-30 (design and installation details). Civil scope: light bases/cans, duct banks, sawcuts, pavement markings (**FAA AC 150/5340-1M**), and coordination with the electrical engineer (series circuits, constant-current regulators). |
| "What drives de-icing facility design?" | Pad sizing and **queuing** (the same D/D/1 logic as PEO B7 Q2), aircraft stand geometry, **glycol collection and containment**, separate drainage and storage, environmental approvals, operations flow. Transport Canada **TP 14052** (ground-icing operations guidelines). |
| "How do you design airside drainage and grading?" | **FAA AC 150/5320-5D** (drainage design), rational method and IDF curves, no ponding on movement areas, TP 312 Ch 3 slope limits, Civil 3D grading. |
| "How do you keep construction safe at an operating airport?" | Phasing, runway/taxiway closures, NOTAMs (via NAV CANADA), temporary displaced thresholds, FOD control, escorts and airside access, a construction safety/phasing plan (**FAA AC 150/5370-2G** is the standard reference). |

### Theme 1: Design delivery and QA/QC

- "Walk me through a project you took from concept to detailed design." Use **STAR**; name the deliverables at each phase.
- "What's in a design report / design basis memo?" Design criteria, standards used (TP 312 / FAA), design aircraft, alternatives, recommendation, risks.
- "How do you QA/QC a drawing and spec package?" Checklists, an independent check, a comment log with close-out, interdisciplinary coordination review, constructability review.
- **Prepare:** one story where your QC caught a real error before tender.

### Theme 2: Schedule and deadlines

- "Explain critical path and float." Forward/backward pass, total float, critical activities. Recovery options: crashing, fast-tracking, re-sequencing. Tools: MS Project or Primavera P6.
- "Airside work windows are only at night. How does that affect the schedule?" Closure windows drive phasing, production rates and cost.
- "Tell me about a time priorities changed suddenly." STAR, with how you re-planned and communicated it.

### Theme 3: Collaboration and communication

- "Tell me about a disagreement with a client, contractor or subconsultant and how you resolved it."
- "Who do you coordinate with on an airport project?" Airport operations, the electrical/lighting discipline, NAV CANADA, airlines/tenants, Transport Canada, the contractor.
- **Prepare:** two STAR stories (one client-facing, one internal team).

### Theme 4: Field reviews and safety

- "What do you check during a paving site review?" Grades and smoothness, density/compaction and mat temperatures, joints, material test results. **This links to your PEO A6 topics:** HMA volumetrics, compaction control and PG binders.
- "Tell me about a safety concern you raised on site." WSP calls HSE a "core paramount value". Have a real example.

### Theme 6: Specs, quantities and cost

- "How do you build a quantity take-off and cost estimate?" Civil 3D surfaces and volumes (earthwork is the **average-end-area** topic from PEO A6), pavement areas by layer, unit rates from past tenders, contingency. Know estimate classes (AACE Class 5→1, or the Canadian **Class D→A** used by federal owners).
- "Which spec formats have you used?" FAA **AC 150/5370-10H** P-items (e.g., P-401 asphalt, P-501 concrete, P-209 base, P-152 excavation), owner/airport-authority specs, and **OPSS** for landside roads.

### Theme 7: Software

- Be ready to describe a Civil 3D workflow: existing surface → alignment/profile → corridor/grading → pipe networks → volumes/QTO → sheets.

### Themes 8–9: Licence and motivation

- "Where are you with your P.Eng.?" State your PEO status plainly: technical exams **A6 (Highway Design, Construction & Maintenance)** and **B7 (Transportation Planning & Engineering)**, the exam date, and your experience record. The posting accepts "eligible", so a clear timeline helps.
- "Why WSP? Why aviation?" Tie your answer to the posting: critical national airport infrastructure, multidisciplinary delivery, growth.
- Salary: the Ontario band is **$81,900–$112,800**. Anchor inside it based on your experience.
